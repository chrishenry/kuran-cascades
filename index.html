When does a crowd tip?

People often hide what they think until enough others speak up first. Timur Kuran showed that public opinion then settles where what people expect matches what they actually do, and that a tiny change in the crowd can send it somewhere else entirely.

How to read it

The curve

Across the bottom is what everyone expects the share of people speaking up to be. The curve shows how many would actually speak up at that expectation, because each person only joins once enough others seem to.

The diagonal

Every point where expectation and reality agree. Off the diagonal, people see a different number than they expected and adjust. The dotted staircase traces those rounds of adjusting.

Resting points and tipping points

Opinion settles where the curve crosses the diagonal from above (solid dot). Where it crosses from below (hollow dot), it tips: start a little to either side and opinion runs away.

The bowl

Kuran's panel B draws the same thing as ground for a ball to roll on. Valleys are resting points. Erase a valley and the ball rolls all the way to the next one. Depths are softened so shallow valleys stay visible.

Why small things matter

Try "Two valleys", then nudge the spark up a few points. Nobody's private views change much, yet opinion leaps from under 10% to nearly everyone. Since people hide their views, nobody can see in advance how close the crowd is to the edge.

Crowd size

Each person has one threshold, so a crowd of 10 moves in 10 point steps and is harder to tip. Larger crowds trace the smooth curve from the book.
